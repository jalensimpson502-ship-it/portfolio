The Challenge

Multiple facilities had different layouts and operating conditions, making it difficult to compare storage practices and identify a common standard. Differing layouts caused confusion between workers and leadership leading to massive cost in lost equipment.

My Approach

I gathered field measurements, built AutoCAD maps, compared layouts and operations, and organized the results so differences between sites could be communicated clearly. For plants I was unable to travel to, I worked hand in hand with contacts at those locations to get the exact data I needed.

Engineering & Iteration

Because each facility had unique constraints, the work required separating local exceptions from opportunities that could reasonably be standardized across the network. On top of that, the analysis was iterative, with feedback from stakeholders helping to refine the recommendations I made to leadership.

Result

The project produced a cross-site view of the current state and recommendations that could be communicated to engineering and leadership stakeholders. Among my findings and analysis, I was able to identify key process issues that could be improved.

What I Learned

I learned that standardization is not about forcing every site to look identical. It is about finding the common engineering principles that remain useful despite the local constraints. Another key thing was going and visiting each facility to gain a deeper understanding of the unique challenges and opportunities as well as better understanding the voice of the customer.
