The Initial Challenge

The original comparison approach did not fully account for outside-air percentage, which affected whether the configurations could be compared fairly.

My Approach

I restructured the analysis around outside-air requirements, emissions rates, equipment configurations, operating assumptions, and regional or facility conditions rather than relying on unit tonnage alone.

Engineering & Iteration

Supplier feedback changed the original "Plan, Do, Check, Act" or PDCA direction, so the analysis was revised rather than defended. That meant revisiting assumptions, reorganizing the comparison logic, and identifying the additional operating inputs needed to convert emissions rates into useful annualized values.

Result

The study became a better engineering decision framework because it reflected how the equipment would actually be applied instead of only comparing nominal capacities. It also provided a very clear picture for stakeholders to understand the tradeoffs and make informed decisions for future projects.

What I Learned

The project reinforced that engineering analysis improves when outside expertise challenges an assumption. Adapting the model is often more valuable than preserving the original plan. Engineering studies benefit from iterative feedback and real-world application.
