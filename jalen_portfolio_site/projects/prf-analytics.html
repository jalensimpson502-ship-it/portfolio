The Challenge

Operational study data contained inconsistent status logic, duplicated fiscal-year labels, missing dates, outliers, and multiple definitions of completion and lateness.

My Approach

I developed cleaned logic, status classifications, fiscal-year handling, late-day categories, completion flags, and dashboard measures intended to make the reporting useful to both individual contributors and leadership. Then, to make data collection and updating easier, I decided to automate the entire process for after my co-op rotation ended.

Engineering & Iteration

The dashboard evolved as edge cases appeared. Using kaizen principles, I refined filters, classification logic, outlier handling, fiscal-year rules, and definitions of open versus completed work so the visualizations reflected operational reality.

Result

The end product made it easier to understand workload, completion behavior, late work, and data-quality gaps while reducing the need to manually interpret raw records.

What I Learned

The biggest lesson was that a good dashboard is really a systems-design problem: the hardest work is often defining trustworthy logic before visualizing anything. In the end, I also learned a lot about the importance of data quality and the iterative nature of dashboard development.
