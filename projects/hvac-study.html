01

The Problem

What needed to be solved?

The first comparison completed before my co-op rotation looked reasonable but did not fully account for outside-air percentage. That meant nominal tonnage alone could create a misleading comparison between equipment configurations.

02

My Approach

How I attacked it.

I revised the model rather than defending the first version. The comparison was rebuilt around how the units would actually operate: outside-air requirements, run time, heat-pump share, gas efficiency, emissions factors, and configuration differences.

03

Impact

Why the work mattered.

The study became a more credible decision framework because it compared real application conditions instead of only nominal equipment sizes, giving stakeholders a clearer way to discuss future standards and tradeoffs.

Model Evolution

The analysis changed when the assumptions met the real system.

The first model established a direction. Technical review exposed where those assumptions did not represent real operating conditions, leading to a more site-responsive decision model.

Qualitative Results

The best configuration depended on more than efficiency alone.

The refined analysis showed that technology selection changes with climate, ventilation requirements, heating demand, fuel cost, and the amount of supplemental heat required.

01

Combustion Heating

Direct Fired

Direct Emissions Higher
Gas Dependency Higher
Initial Cost Lower

Simple and efficient heating, but application depends heavily on sufficient outside-air requirements.

02

Separated Combustion

Indirect Fired

Direct Emissions Reduced
Gas Dependency Higher
Initial Cost Moderate

Separates combustion products from the supply air, but adds components, maintenance, and system complexity.

03

Electrified Heating

Heat Pump

Direct Emissions Low
Gas Dependency Lower
Initial Cost Higher

Reduces supplemental gas-heating demand, but performance and economics remain sensitive to climate and operating conditions.

Takeaway

The biggest takeaway from this project was that a study can always be more detailed. The goal is to limit assumptions in order to continuously improve the decision-making process. The results of this project demonstrated the value of iterative analysis and further stakeholder engagement.
